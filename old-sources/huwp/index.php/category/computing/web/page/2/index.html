Flock, again

Posted in Web at 11:00 am by leingang

I’ve been using Flock on a trial basis for a while now. A friend told me since it was Firefox-based it would be faster on a mac. Plus he’s into all that Web 2.0 stuff that I’m still getting into. But I’m starting to get the hang of both.

Flock aims to integrate popular web sites and web activities into your browser. It has a blogging tool built in (I’m using it now). Along with the canonical bookmark (Flock calls them “favorites”) management utility, Flock gives a nice interface with your del.icio.us or shadows accounts so you can tag pages on-the-fly. If you’re into Flickr or photobucket, you can use Flock to manage your photo collection.

It also has a nice clipboard of web “snippets” that lives in the bottom margin of any Flock window. So if you’re putting together a blog post and you want to copy an image from another location, drag it down there and then into anything else:

I guess your interest in Flock depends on how much you use these other applications. But I think it will be making it easier for me to blog, which is something I’m going to try to do more of.

PS I should give a shoutout to Damon who was the first to tell me about Flock.

04.20.07

Web pedagogy seminar: Google Earth continues to rock

Posted in Web at 1:55 pm by leingang

I just got back from a monthly seminar on web pedagogies.  The majority of the seminar was spent on an eye-popping demonstration of Google Earth by Jason Ur.  Jason is an anthropologist (and only three years out of his Ph.D. is already tired of the joke about his name and his field) who uses Google Earth in his classes to visit and really “see” archaeological sites.  I was able to find Kirkuk just by typing it in.  Very cool.

The rest of it was about social bookmarking and how it can improve university library information services. Students today expect Web 2.0 technology from educational websites.  Which means I should be learning more about http://del.icio.us/ and the like.

I’m still trying to figure out how to get collaborative web technologies into mathematics courses.  Unlike many disciplines, we don’t have primary texts (mostly we don’t.  I recall a few brilliant mathematicians like Jean-Pierre Serre whose theses spawned seminars).  We have our objects and the relationships between them.

I was thinking it would be fun to use a wiki for a Moore-method style mathematics course.  The wiki would be only open to students in the course, and the instructor could seed it with problems.  Then the course would consist of “filling out” the wiki and using the discussion features (such as on a MediaWiki site) to question and solidify the proofs.  There are the usual barriers to that: mathematicians stubbornly refuse to limit themselves to notation expressible by HTML and a latin character set.  Wikis allow TeX encoding but that’s kind of a steep learning curve.  But if you can train up your students on the software, maybe it could work.  Or, since the Moore method is all about constructing your own mathematics, maybe they would adapt to an a more ASCII-friendly mathematics notation.

10.10.06

Flock

Posted in WordPress, Web at 8:38 am by leingang

I just got pointed to Flock by a friend over the weekend. It’s pretty cool. It’s a web browser with support for many of the popular web applications (read “Web 2.0″) built in.

Like I was telling him, me and Web 2.0–not so much. But I like to blog, and want to blog more. This application has a built-in blog

editor, so hitting command-B just calls up a window to make new posts. Right-click on links in web pages, and you can create a new blog page with that link in it.

Other things I haven’t got into yet include support for Flickr and Gmail. You can open up a new message and drag photos into it to send pictures to friends. Pretty cool, if I didn’t have so much work to do…

And from the department of weird coincidences, my friend was telling me about Flock while we were dining at the Union Oyster House in Boston. While stepping outside for a smoke my friend bumped into a guy with a Flock T-Shirt on. He was one of the lead developers!
