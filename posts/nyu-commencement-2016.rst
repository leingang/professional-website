NYU's university-wide commencement was today at Yankee Stadium.  Although I am a
Red Sox fan, I often go and I usually enjoy myself.

It was an adventure alone to get there.  My NJTransit bus got stuck in the bottleneck
where 495 comes off of the Turnpike.  I got into Port Authority a half hour late and
barely made it to Washington Square in time to run to my office, put on my robe, and
get on the bus.

It takes about an hour to get from NYU to the stadium.  I chatted with `Rick
Garcia
<http://www.nyu.edu/life/diversity-nyu/diversity-resources/faculty-resources/postdoctoral-and-transition/fellows7.html>`__,
an assistant professor and  diversity fellow at the NYU Rory Myers College of
Nursing. We swapped stories of outrageous things students have done, and
wondering if they were innocent and unaware or playing us for fools (I'm pretty
sure it's some from each category).

Once there I met up with `Vindya Bhat <http://math.nyu.edu/~vbhat/>`__, another
one of the Clinical Faculty who I convinced to come along.  We arrived at the
deliveries entrance to the stadium, and we processed from the outfield entrance,
along the warning track, and to our seats at third base.

.. media:: https://instagram.com/p/BFjYkdAOWVj/?taken-by=mleingang

All around us were students in violet robes, probably 15,000 of them: bachelor's degree
candidates, master's, Ph.D.s, doctors, lawyers, dentists, and engineers.  It's easy to
forget as one class passes through and another takes its place, that for these students
this day is the most important of their lives so far.

Among the honorary degree recipients were Billy Crystal.  He is a lifelong New
Yorker and wore a Yankees cap with a tassel.  It was not mentioned in his
citation that in one of his best movies (When Harry Met Sally) he portrayed an
alumnus of the University of Chicago.

John Lewis was also honored.  He received a standing ovation from the students,
and duly so.  Another honoree was Hillary Margaret Marshall, who recently
retired as chief justice of the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court.  She made
sure that her citation read that she decreed a Red Sox World Series championship
this year.

Andy Hamilton has been president of NYU for less than five months, but plays the
part very well.  In his address, he said:

    Before NYU I was head of Oxford, where we do our commencements a bit
    differently.  It takes place in the 350-year-old `Sheldonian Theatre`_.  All
    faculty and degree candidates are in white tie.  And the entire ceremony is
    conducted in Latin.

    Today I am standing on second base in Yankee Stadium, where the last time
    Latin was spoken was when the pope said mass here.  I can smell the popcorn
    and the hot dogs from the stands, I can hear the rumbling of the subway
    behind me, and I say to myself: there is no place I'd rather be.

He said many other nice things, too, including advising the graduates to make
the most of their inevitable setbacks.  He shared the story of not getting
tenure on his first review (For non-academic readers, this means you are fired and
you have to start over someplace else).  But he eventually got another position,
made tenure and promotions, became head of the department, and went up the
ladder.

It was about three hours from marching in to filing out, up the aisle, out the
gate, and onto Jerome Street.  We took the same shuttle bus back to NYU, which
meant another hour in traffic.  The other option would have been riding the
subway in my regalia, which I've done and will probably do again next time.  Along
with wearing my Red Sox cap.
